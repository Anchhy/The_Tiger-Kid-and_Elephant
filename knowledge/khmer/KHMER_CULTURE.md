# Khmer Culture & Heritage Guide for *The Tiger, Kid, and Elephant*

> **Purpose**: Authentic Cambodian architectural, textile, botanical, and symbolic references so the game's maze maps, character art, and educational content reflect real Khmer culture rather than generic jungle fantasy.

---

## 1. Angkorian Temple & Maze Architecture

| Cultural Element | Khmer Term | Architectural & Symbolic Role | Game Design Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gopura** | `ខ្លោងទ្វារប្រាសាទ` | Monumental cruciform temple gateway marking enclosure entrances, crowned with lotus-bud towers and narrative pediments. | Left Tiger entrance gate and right Elephant exit sanctuary gate. |
| **Seven-Headed Naga** | `នាគក្បាល ៧` | Mythic multi-headed serpent balustrades lining Angkorian causeways and stairs, symbolizing the sacred bridge between heaven and earth. | Guardian balustrades flanking the Elephant exit stairs (replaces generic 1-headed snakes). |
| **Kbach** | `ក្បាច់` | Traditional Cambodian ornamental motifs inspired by leaves, flowers, and flames (e.g., *kbach angkor*, *kbach phka chan*, *kbach ampil*). | Borders on maze walls, *Gopura* pediments, and glowing puzzle checkpoint steles (no garbled pseudo-text). |
| **Sandstone** | `ថ្មភក់` | Fine-grained stone from Phnom Kulen used for sculpted lintels, bas-reliefs, and sacred sanctuaries. | Carved upper maze wall panels, Naga statues, and puzzle steles. |
| **Laterite** | `ថ្មបាយក្រៀម` | Porous, iron-rich reddish-brown stone used as durable structural foundations and outer temple enclosures. | Lower maze wall foundations and stepping blocks. |
| **Spung Tree Roots** | `ដើមស្ពុង` | Giant roots (*Tetrameles nudiflora*) famously enveloping temple masonry at Ta Prohm and Preah Khan. | Atmospheric framing over the left Tiger entrance and outer ruins. |
| **Terrace of the Elephants** | `ព្រះលានជល់ដំរី` | Late 12th-century Angkor Thom royal platform carved with life-sized elephants and mahouts under King Jayavarman VII. | Inspiration for the Elephant exit courtyard and carved elephant wall reliefs. |
| **Bayon & Banteay Srei** | `ប្រាសាទបាយ័ន` / `បន្ទាយស្រី` | Bayon preserves daily-life reliefs (including the *krama*); Banteay Srei showcases delicate pink-sandstone *kbach* carving. | Cultural reference for daily-life reliefs and ornate doorway trim. |

## 2. Authentic Cambodian Traditional Dress & Daily Identity

- **Krama (`ក្រមា`)**:
  - Traditional checkerboard-woven cotton or silk cloth (commonly red/white or blue/white), inscribed by **UNESCO** on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (*"Cultural practices and expressions linked to Krama, a traditional woven textile in Cambodia"*).
  - Depicted on Bayon bas-reliefs and used daily across Cambodia as a scarf, head wrap, belt, baby carrier, or sun shield.
  - **Game usage**: Draped around the Kid Explorer's neck/shoulders for unmistakable Cambodian identity.
- **Sampot (`សំពត់`) & Sampot Chang Kben (`សំពត់ចងក្បិន`)**:
  - Cambodia's national lower garment; the *Chang Kben* wraps around the waist, passes between the legs, and tucks into the back waistband like knee-length trousers—historically seen in Angkorian reliefs and traditional dance/formal wear, and practical for movement.
  - **Game usage**: Brown cotton *Sampot Chang Kben* paired with a simple off-white cotton shirt and explorer backpack.

## 3. Cambodian National Symbols & Wildlife

- **Kind Cambodian Elephant (`ដំរី` *damrei*)**:
  - Revered in Cambodian Buddhist, Hindu, and royal tradition (symbolizing dignity, stability, peace, strength, and good fortune; celebrated at Angkor Thom's Terrace of the Elephants).
  - **Game usage**: Adorned in a saffron-gold and crimson royal brocade caparison (`ភួយដំរី`) with *kbach* motifs, lotus-petal fringe, and a golden Cambodian forehead crown ornament.
- **Rumduol (`ផ្ការំដួល`)**:
  - Cambodia's official national flower (*Sphaerocoryne clavipes*), recognized for its small pale creamy-yellow blossoms and sweet evening fragrance—symbolizing grace and Cambodian heritage.
  - **Game usage**: Subtle pale-yellow *Rumduol* flowers alongside pink lotus (`ផ្កាឈូក`) near temple steps and foliage.
- **Tiger (`ខ្លា` *klaa*)**:
  - Represents wild forest danger (`ព្រៃភ្នំ`) that motivates the Kid to solve Khmer word puzzles carefully and reach safety.

## 4. Cultural Accuracy Rules for Visual Assets & UI

1. **No garbled pseudo-Khmer text in illustrations**: Painted stone steles and map scrolls must use pure **Kbach** geometric/lotus patterns unless real, verified Khmer Unicode is rendered cleanly by Flutter.
2. **Use authentic 7-headed Khmer Naga balustrades** (`នាគក្បាល ៧`) rather than generic Western/fantasy dragons or single-headed cobras.
3. **Preserve respectful portrayal** of royal regalia, temple carvings, and everyday Cambodian clothing (*krama* and *sampot*).
